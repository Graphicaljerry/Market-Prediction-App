How to read the tracker

Every panel and number, in plain English. The app predicts one thing: at the end of each 15-minute round, will the price be OVER the line or UNDER it — the same bet Robinhood/Kalshi offer.

The whole app in 10 seconds
↑ OVER — finished above the line ↓ UNDER — finished below the line · SKIP — no clear edge, sit out

Green = up / OVER. Red = down / UNDER. Amber = skip. That colour code is used everywhere — the price line, the pick, the arrows, the glow behind the cards.

②Recent-15-min arrows

A row of past rounds — did each one finish OVER or UNDER its line. This should match the little arrows on Robinhood.

RECENT 15-MIN
↓↑↓ ↑↑↓ ↑↑
  • Oldest on the left, newest on the right. ↑ = OVER, ↓ = UNDER.
  • Solid arrows are confirmed against Kalshi's own settled result — guaranteed to match Robinhood.
  • A dim, dashed arrow (↑) is a rough estimate from a candle (used only to fill old gaps), not a graded outcome.
  • A round can briefly show "settling" in Recent Rounds — it's graded provisionally on Coinbase until Kalshi confirms it (a beat or two later). Once confirmed it never changes.

Why might it differ from Robinhood for a minute? Kalshi settles on a 60-second average of a multi-exchange index; a single Coinbase price can land on the other side of a razor-thin round. The app fixes itself to Kalshi's real result within a cycle — so "settling" = "not final yet."

⚠️ Why 100% isn't magic — read this

The tracker SKIPs every coin-flip round and only bets the easy, lopsided ones. A SKIP counts for nothing — it isn't graded or scored. So "10 of 10" means 10 for 10 on the bets it chose, not 10 of every round. That's why the coverage line matters:

Bet 21% of 47 rounds = it only bet 1 in 5 rounds (very selective). 53% if it bet them all = its honest hit-rate if it had bet every round — that's the number that shows whether the skipping actually adds an edge. A poker player who folds every weak hand also "wins 100%."

⑩Key ideas & glossary

SKIP

No clear edge — sit this round out. The 24/7 tracker SKIPs most rounds on purpose; a skip is never scored.

Confluence

When several independent reads (crowd, momentum, order book, the learned model) point the same way. The tracker only commits when they agree — that's the only real edge on a near-random market.

Favorite-longshot bias

A well-documented market quirk: heavy favorites are slightly under-priced (they win a touch more than their price implies) and cheap longshots are over-priced. The app leans gently into clear favorites.

Panic-fade

Moderate moves tend to continue, but a violent, extreme spike usually snaps back. So the app rides normal drift but fades the over-reaction.

Confirmed vs settling

Confirmed = graded against Kalshi's own settled result (matches Robinhood, never changes). Settling = a provisional Coinbase grade, waiting on Kalshi.

Coverage & shadow hit-rate

Coverage / bet-rate = how often it actually bets vs skips. "If it bet every round" = its hit-rate including the rounds it sat out (shadow-graded) — the honest test of whether skipping helps.

The learned model

A tiny model that learns, per coin, how the round-open signals map to OVER/UNDER. 15-minute direction is near-random, so it's a faint, calibrated tilt, never a crystal ball.

Alerts 🔔

Turn on alerts (AI Co-Pilot setup, or the Worker's ntfy push) to get pinged only on high-confidence, non-SKIP picks — a coin it's willing to bet and several reads agree.

Predicting 15-minute crypto direction is close to a coin-flip — these tools are faint, honest tilts and disciplined skipping, not a money printer. For tracking only · not financial advice.
